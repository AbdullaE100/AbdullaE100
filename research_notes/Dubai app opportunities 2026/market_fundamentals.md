## 1. Population, demographics, income and wealth (Dubai first, UAE second)

### Takeaway
Dubai had about 4.81 million residents at end-September 2026, after adding about 332,000 people (+7.5%) in 2025 and about 227,000 in the first nine months of 2026. The population is heavily male (68.5%) and prime-working-age (largest band 30–34). The UAE has about 11.4 million residents (UN, Oct 2025), about 89% of them expatriates, led by South Asians. Wealth inflows are the largest in the world (Henley forecast of 9,800 net millionaires in 2025), but most workers are low-wage: one unverified report says more than 60% earn under AED 5,000 a month. That gives a "barbell" market: a large mass segment with little money and a small, fast-growing affluent segment.

### Inferences
- Dubai's 2026 growth pace: 226,885 people in about 272 days (1 Jan–29 Sept) is about 830 per day, or roughly 300,000 a year annualised, slightly below 2025's about 910 per day. Dubai is adding a city of about 0.3m net new residents a year. Gross arrivals are much higher: H1 2026 alone saw about 1.05m new residence permits in Dubai, many of them replacing departures.
- Dubai is about 42% of the UAE population (4.8m of about 11.4m; different dates and sources).
- The core addressable adult audience: about 81.5% of 11.4m is about 9.3m adults in the UAE, and the 25–44 band (52.5%) is about 6.0m. In Dubai the ratio is about 2.2 men per woman, so female-targeted apps face a structurally smaller base (about 1.44m women in Dubai).
- Nationality estimates are inflated. The table above sums to well over 11.4m (India + Pakistan + Bangladesh + Emiratis alone is about 8.5m), so embassy figures probably include non-residents or are stale. Use them as relative rank (India far ahead, then Pakistan, Bangladesh, Philippines, Iran, Nepal, Egypt, China), not absolutes. South Asians are probably 50–60% of residents. Arabic speakers (Emiratis plus Arab expats) are roughly 25–30%. Western expats (UK, France, Canada, US, Australia and others) are probably under 5%.
- The blue-collar majority (low wages, cash-heavy, quick wage withdrawal) and the affluent segment (Golden Visa holders, HNWIs, Western and Indian professionals) have very different willingness to pay. Premium B2C subscriptions realistically target perhaps 2–3m higher-income residents. Mass-market apps need ad, remittance-adjacent or very low price points.

### Gaps
- No official 2025/2026 FCSC UAE total with an Emirati/non-Emirati split, and no official nationality breakdown (the UAE does not publish one).
- No verified UAE-wide Golden Visa total. Blog figures range from about 160k to 650k and are unsourced.
- No verified share of blue-collar vs white-collar workers. A secondary claim that the 2024 Labour Force Survey shows an average gross salary of AED 16,000 and a median of AED 10,000 appeared in search snippets, but I could not verify the original source.
- No published 2024 Dubai HIES income bands. The Henley 2026 report (published mid-2026) was not retrieved, so a 2026 HNWI inflow figure is missing.

## 2. Smartphone and internet penetration, iOS vs Android, time in apps, most-used apps and AI adoption

### Takeaway
The UAE is saturated and hyper-connected: 99% internet penetration, 23.0m mobile connections (202% of population), about 97% smartphone penetration, and the world's fastest median mobile download speed. Residents spend about 8 hours a day on screens (#1 globally on TRG's Tech Dependency Index). Android dominates by volume (about 73–78% of web traffic) and iOS holds about 22–27%. The UAE leads the world in generative-AI adoption (73.3% of working-age people), and ChatGPT was the #1 free iPhone app in the UAE in 2025.

- Cellular mobile connections: 23.0m (202% of population), +1.7m (+8.1%) YoY (GSMA Intelligence) — [DataReportal Digital 2026 UAE](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-united-arab-emirates)
- Median mobile download speed 614.42 Mbps (+54.2% YoY); fixed 327.56 Mbps (Ookla) — [DataReportal Digital 2026 UAE](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-united-arab-emirates)
- For comparison, Digital 2025 reported 21.9m connections (195% of population) — [DataReportal Digital 2025 UAE](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-united-arab-emirates)
- Smartphone penetration about 97% (AppsFlyer H1 2025 report coverage; search summary) — [Campaign ME, 2025](https://campaignme.com/chinese-retail-apps-driving-73-per-cent-of-uae-e-commerce-ad-spend-report/)

**AI adoption (Microsoft AI Economy Institute; share of people aged 15–64 who used a generative-AI product during the period)**

Ranked #1 globally, more than 4x the global average of 17.8% (Q1 2026) — [The National, 25 Sept 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/09/25/uae-pulls-ahead-in-global-ranking-on-ai-adoption/); [Gulf News, 2025](https://gulfnews.com/technology/uae-tops-global-rankings-in-ai-adoption-in-the-workplace-microsoft-report-1.500330512)

### Inferences
- If iOS holds about 22–27% of mobile web traffic among about 11.3m internet users, the iOS base is roughly 2.5–3.0m users. It is probably concentrated in higher-income segments, given iOS's lower fraud and more resilient UA spend. iOS-first monetisation is reasonable for premium subscriptions, while Android covers mass reach (South Asian blue-collar and middle-income users).
- Very high AI adoption, ChatGPT at #1, and Gemini in the top 10 point to an educated, AI-literate user base willing to try AI-native consumer apps, which supports the founder's AI-app track record.
- The paid charts (VPN/proxy, prayer times, study/flashcard, GPS tools) show UAE users pay up front for utility, religion and study tools.
- Penetration is saturated, so growth comes from population inflow (about 300k a year into Dubai) and share capture, not new-to-internet users. Marketers report that saturation is real (see Section 3).

### Gaps
- No 2025/2026 UAE-specific "daily time in apps" figure from data.ai/Sensor Tower, and no UAE ranking of the most-used apps by monthly active users. DataReportal's web page text omits the deck's app-ranking slides.
- No authoritative installed-base OS split. Statcounter measures web pageviews, not devices.

## 3. Consumer app spending, top categories and subscription benchmarks (with AED price points)

### Takeaway
GCC in-app purchase (IAP) revenue topped US$700M in Q2 2025 (+20% YoY vs 13% globally), and the UAE had the region's fastest revenue growth (+26% YoY). AI/Software (+77%), Financial Services (+57%) and Shopping (+35%) grew fastest. Media & Entertainment, Software, Jobs & Education and Health & Wellness are the largest grossing genres. RevenueCat's 2026 benchmarks show MEA converts worse than Western markets (trial-to-paid median about 19.5% vs 34.2% in North America) and is the only region with negative median MRR growth (−9.7%). Health & Fitness remains the best-monetising subscription category globally. UAE AI subscriptions are priced at about AED 19–80 a month.

### Inferences
- UAE IAP size (my estimate, low confidence): the GCC runs at about US$700M a quarter, or about US$2.8bn a year gross. If the UAE is 25–35% of GCC IAP (it has about 20% of GCC population but higher per-capita spend; I found no published share), UAE gross IAP would be about US$0.7–1.0bn a year, or about US$60–90 per resident. This needs verification from Sensor Tower/AppMagic.
- AED price ladder: the AED is pegged at about 3.6725 per USD (general knowledge). A US$9.99 tier is about AED 37; $39.99/year is about AED 147; ChatGPT Plus at AED 79.99 is about US$21.8, which suggests UAE store prices include 5% VAT and sit at or slightly above US list prices. Typical consumer tiers: AED 15–40 a month, AED 99–199 a year for wellness/education, and AED 30–80 a month for AI tools.
- MEA funnel maths (RevenueCat): about 3.3% download-to-trial × 19.5% trial-to-paid is about 0.6% download-to-paid through the trial path, roughly a third of North America's rate. For a bootstrapped founder this argues for hard paywalls (10.7% vs 2.1% D35 conversion globally), annual plans, Health & Fitness or Education positioning, and targeting high-income UAE iOS users rather than "MEA" broadly. The negative MEA MRR growth figure reflects MEA-headquartered developers, not UAE consumer demand, so read it with care.
- Categories with UAE/GCC tailwinds that match the founder's portfolio: AI/Software (+77% IAP), Health & Wellness (+17% IAP, +14% downloads), Jobs & Education (+16% IAP), Religion & Spirituality (+14% IAP; Salatuk in the paid top 10). Finance (+57% IAP, +34% downloads) is large but regulated.

### Gaps
- No public 2025 or 2026 UAE-only total for App Store + Google Play consumer spend, revenue per user, or global rank. Statista's UAE App outlook values are paywalled, and Sensor Tower/data.ai/AppMagic/Appfigures UAE totals were not found publicly.
- No public 2025 UAE top-grossing list by category.
- RevenueCat does not report a UAE-specific or GCC-specific breakout. No UAE renewal-rate data (monthly or annual) was found. Adapty's 2026 benchmark was not retrieved for MENA data.

## 4. Language use in apps and the Arabic-content gap

### Takeaway
English is the de facto app language for the expat majority, while Arabic speakers (Emiratis plus Arab expats) are roughly a quarter to a third of residents. Arabic is badly under-served online: often-cited estimates put it at under 3% of web content versus about 5% of the world's population. Abu Dhabi's culture chief said in Sept 2026 that 80% of the content children consume is not in Arabic. There is no hard data on app usage by language in the UAE.

### Cited Findings
- Native Arabic speakers are about 5% of the world population, but Arabic content is below 3% of online content — [UN ESCWA Digital Arabic Content Award (WSA)](https://wsa-global.org/un-escwa-digital-arabic-content-award/)
- Arabic is used by only about 1.1% of the top 10 million websites, while Arabic speakers are about 3.5% of the world population (date of article uncertain, older) — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/1835056)
- An undated blog estimate puts Arabic speakers at about 5% of internet users and Arabic at 0.6% of content — [Al Sheikh Media blog](https://alsheikhmedia.pages.dev/en/blog/the-arabic-content-gap/)
- "80 per cent of the content consumed by children is currently in languages other than Arabic." He proposed a 50-50 split as a target. Arabic has about 430m speakers worldwide, forecast to exceed 750m by 2050 — [Khaleej Times, 20 Sept 2026 (Mohamed Khalifa Al Mubarak, DCT Abu Dhabi chairman)](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/arabic-content-gap-80-percent-foreign-media-kids-consume)
- Arabic-language and Islamic apps monetise well in the region. Shahid (Arabic streaming) was #2 by GCC IAP revenue in H1 2025, behind TikTok and ahead of ChatGPT. GCC Religion & Spirituality IAP grew 14% — [Sensor Tower & Bidease, Oct 2025](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/ST_2025-Middle-East-App-Growth-Report.pdf)
- Salatuk (prayer times) was #6 paid iPhone app and Tarbaa (Arabic trivia) #6 paid game in the UAE in 2025. Jackaroo King (a Gulf board game) was in the free-games top 10 — [The National, Dec 2025](https://thenational-the-national-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/future/technology/2025/12/13/chatgpt-was-most-downloaded-free-app-in-uae-in-2025-apple-says); [tbreak, Dec 2025](https://tbreak.com/apple-app-store-uae-most-downloaded-apps-2025/)
- Dubai's government prayer app Dulook DXB neared 1m downloads and saw a 185% usage surge in Ramadan, with over 35,000 concurrent users at Maghrib/Fajr peaks (year of release not confirmed) — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/1749dxv-dubai’s-dulook-dxb-prayer-app-nears-one-million)

### Inferences
- From the nationality estimates in Section 1: Arabic speakers are about 3.0–3.5m (Emiratis about 1.3m plus about 2m Arab expats), or about 25–30% of residents. South Asian languages (Hindi, Urdu, Malayalam, Bengali, Nepali) are spoken by about 50–60%, and Tagalog by about 6%. English is the shared lingua franca for most commercial apps. Bilingual English/Arabic is table stakes for government-adjacent and family/religious apps. Hindi/Urdu localisation could reach the largest but lowest-paying segment.
- The Arabic gap is most visible in children's content and education. The founder's Quran app and IELTS examiner sit on two sides of this: Arabic/Islamic content demand, and English-proficiency demand from the expat and Arab workforce.

### Gaps
- No UAE data on app usage or app-store language settings by language (English vs Arabic vs Hindi/Urdu/Tagalog). No 2025/2026 authoritative measure of Arabic's share of web or app content; most figures are from 2013–2018 or unsourced.

## 5. Payment behaviour: cards, wallets, BNPL, cash, salaries and cost of living

### Takeaway
The UAE is now about 80% digital by payment count (Visa, 2025 fieldwork). Mobile/wallet payments are 21% of transactions, and about 7 in 10 residents are more willing to use Apple Pay/Google Pay/Samsung Wallet than cash or cards. Cash survives in tips, remittances, rent and the low-income segment. Bank account ownership is about 94%. BNPL is mainstream: Tabby reports 25m registered users and an annualised volume of US$18bn across the GCC, with a valuation of US$6.5bn (Sept 2026). Rents rose 20%+ in affordable communities in 2025 while salaries stayed flat, which squeezes the middle segment.

### Cited Findings
**Digital vs cash**
- Visa (released Jan 2026; online survey of 1,200 across the GCC, 2025 fieldwork): 80% of payments are made digitally. 68% of UAE consumers are "largely non-cash" (+7 points). Mobile payments are 21% of transactions. Cash for everyday purchases fell to 16% from 25%. Cash use in local markets fell 19%, taxis 18% and bills 12%. Digital P2P rose 5 points to 35%. Remaining cash uses: tips (58% pay in cash), international transfers through exchange houses (25%) and rent (15%) — [Visa UAE newsroom, Jan 2026](https://www.visa.com/en-ae/newsroom/uae-digital-payments-adoption-insights)
- Visa "Where Cash Hides" (Jan 2025; UAE n=600; fieldwork Aug 2024): 23% of consumer transactions were still cash. P2P was 33% cash (vs 43% in 2023) and everyday spending 25% (vs 27%) — [Visa UAE newsroom, Jan 2025](https://www.visa.com/en-ae/newsroom/uae-cash-usage-digital-payments-insights)
- SixthFactor (n=1,050, 2025–26): 70.7% are more willing to use Apple Pay, Google Pay or Samsung Wallet over cash or physical cards. Adoption is higher in wealthier households and lower among lower-income, less-educated groups — [Fintech News Middle East](https://fintechnews.ae/32311/payments/uae-digital-wallet-adoption-sixthfactor/); see also [TechAfrica News, 26 June 2026](https://techafricanews.com/2026/06/26/seven-in-ten-uae-consumers-prefer-digital-wallets-over-cash-and-cards-survey-finds/)
- 94% of UAE residents hold a personal bank account, up from 85% in 2021 (World Bank Findex) (YouGov for Daleel). 50% are uncertain about their credit card choice — [Khaleej Times, 12 May 2025](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/surge-in-bank-account-ownerships-positions-uae-among-most-banked-nations)
- Low-income workers: cash dependence fell from 84% to 69% in two years, and about 45% withdraw wages within 24 hours (Edenred 2025) — [The Global Filipino Magazine](https://theglobalfilipinomagazine.com/?p=57908)

**BNPL**
- Tabby, Feb 2025 (Series E, US$160M at a US$3.3bn valuation): more than 15m registered users, 40,000+ sellers and US$10bn+ annualised volume — [Tabby press release, Feb 2025](https://tabby.ai/en-AE/press/series-e-funding)
- Tabby, Sept 2026 (Series F, US$233M at a US$6.5bn valuation): over US$18bn annualised transaction volume, 25m registered users and 70,000 business partners, profitable since 2023. The deal is subject to Saudi Central Bank approval and a Saudi IPO is pending — [Wamda, Sept 2026](https://www.wamda.com/2026/09/tabby-hits-6-5-billion-valuation-233-million-series-f)
- Tamara partnered with Amazon Payment Services (July 2025) to expand BNPL across the UAE and Saudi Arabia — [Tamara](https://tamara.co/en-AE/tamara-partners-with-amazon-to-expand-bnpl-in-uae-saudi)
- GCC proxy: Consumer Banking and Digital Wallets lead finance-app adoption. Financial Services downloads +34% and IAP +57% (H1 2025) — [Sensor Tower & Bidease, Oct 2025](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/ST_2025-Middle-East-App-Growth-Report.pdf)

**Cost-of-living pressure**
- Bayut 2025: affordable apartment rents rose more than 20% in several Dubai communities, and budget villa rents rose up to 24% — [Khaleej Times, Jan 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/affordable-rentals-surge-past-20-as-dubai-property-market-looks-to-sustain-healthier-pace-in-2026)
- Betterhomes survey (n=1,439): 55% of Dubai tenants plan to buy within 1–3 years, up from 25% the year before — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/property/dubai-rent-hike-pushes-tenants-buy-homes)
- Cooper Fitch: average salaries were projected to stay flat in 2025, and about 26% of businesses planned no raises (search summary) — [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/as-expats-flock-to-dubai-living-costs-are-surging-salaries-not-so-much/)
- Fewer than 31% of UAE residents show basic financial understanding (industry estimate) — [The Global Filipino Magazine](https://theglobalfilipinomagazine.com/?p=57908)

### Inferences
- App-store billing works well in the UAE: high card and wallet penetration (Apple Pay is ubiquitous; 21% of transactions are mobile) means little payment friction for subscriptions among banked middle- and high-income users. The blue-collar segment is still semi-cash and withdraws wages fast, which limits recurring subscriptions there.
- BNPL ubiquity (Tabby 25m registered GCC users) shows residents are comfortable splitting purchases. For B2B or marketplace models, Tabby/Tamara integration is an expected checkout option for higher-ticket services.
- Rising rents with flat pay argue for tools that save or make money (budgeting, deals, rent/bill splitting, side-income) and against premium-priced discretionary subscriptions for mid-income residents.

### Gaps
- No Global Findex 2025 UAE debit or credit card ownership rates were found. No UAE-specific Apple Pay share of card transactions. No verified UAE-only BNPL user count; Tabby and Tamara report GCC-wide figures, and Tamara's 20m vs 14m user claims conflict and are undated. Market-research BNPL sizes (e.g. about US$1.17bn UAE 2025) are low-quality and not cited.
- No official 2025 Dubai CPI figure was retrieved, and no official salary-band distribution.

## 6. Government strategy that shapes demand

### Takeaway
Dubai's D33 agenda (launched Jan 2023) aims to double the economy by 2033, with AED 32 trillion in cumulative economic targets and AED 100bn a year from digital transformation. The Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI (2024) targets the same AED 100bn a year and a 50% productivity gain. The UAE Digital Economy Strategy aims to double the digital economy's share of GDP from 9.7% to 19.4% by 2031 (about 12% reported in 2026). Federal plans to run half of government services on AI agents and to teach AI from K-12 create B2G/B2B and edtech tailwinds.

### Cited Findings
- D33, launched 4 Jan 2023: double Dubai's economy by 2033, with total economic targets of AED 32 trillion (about US$8.7tn) over 10 years. Digital transformation is to add an average of AED 100bn a year. FDI is to average AED 60bn a year (from AED 32bn). Private-sector investment target is AED 1tn by 2033. About 100 transformative projects; productivity +50% — [WAM, Jan 2023](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/hszrg6z2-mohammed-bin-rashid-launches-dubai-economic-agenda); [Invest in Dubai, D33](https://investindubai.gov.ae/en/why-dubai/d33-agenda); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2226701/business-economy)
- Dubai Universal Blueprint for AI (launched by Sheikh Hamdan, 2024): contribute AED 100bn a year to Dubai's economy and raise productivity 50% through digital solutions, in support of D33. Chief AI Officers in all government entities, AI and Web3 incubators, an AI Week in schools, and annual review — [UAE Government portal u.ae](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/strategies-initiatives-and-awards/strategies-plans-and-visions/Ai/dubai-universal-blueprint-for-ai); [Gulf News, 2024](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/sheikh-hamdan-announces-launch-of-dubai-universal-blueprint-for-artificial-intelligence-1.1714404361861)
- UAE Digital Economy Strategy (Cabinet, 2022): raise the digital economy from 9.7% to 19.4% of GDP within 10 years (non-oil GDP share 11.7% baseline) — [Khaleej Times, 2022](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/government/uae-new-digital-economy-strategy-to-double-gdp-contribution-to-19-4-in-10-years)
- The digital economy now contributes "close to 12%" of GDP, with a target of more than 20% by 2031 — [Khaleej Times, May 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business-technology-review/uae-bets-on-artificial-intelligence-supremacy-to-power-digital-future)
- Dubai Chamber of Digital Economy projected the UAE digital economy growing from US$38bn to US$140bn by 2031 — [Gulf Business, 2023](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2023/economy/uaes-digital-economy-to-reach-140bn-in-2031)
- Federal AI push (2026): half of federal government services to be delivered via AI agents within two years; 80,000 federal employees to be trained in agentic AI; 35,000 Abu Dhabi government staff given Microsoft Copilot; an AI curriculum approved for public and private schools K-12; 22,000 teachers to be trained in AI — [The National, 25 Sept 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/09/25/uae-pulls-ahead-in-global-ranking-on-ai-adoption/)

### Inferences
- These policies create demand for AI-enabled B2B/B2G tools (government vendors, SME digitisation, AI training) and edtech (AI literacy for K-12 and teachers). A solo founder will find it hard to win government contracts directly but can sell to the SMEs and schools being pushed to adopt AI. Population growth targets embedded in D33 (and the Dubai 2040 urban plan, not retrieved) sustain an inflow of new residents who need onboarding apps (visas, housing, jobs, language, community).

### Gaps
- The UAE National Strategy for AI 2031 targets were not retrieved separately. No quantified consumer-app procurement or subsidies (e.g. grants for app startups such as Dubai Future Foundation or in5 programmes) were researched within this scope.

## 7. Seasonality: summer exodus, Ramadan, and the winter tourist and event influx

### Takeaway
Dubai's tourism peaks in winter (record 2.04m visitors in December 2025; January 2025 1.94m) and hit 19.59m overnight visitors in 2025. In Ramadan, UAE installs rise about 10%, sessions about 9%, and shopping and e-commerce surge (UAE shopping sessions +111%). Food delivery dips during the fast and recovers at Eid. Summer (July–August) sees a resident exodus with school holidays, but there is no hard data on its size. The official population count keeps rising through summer because it counts residency, not presence.

### Cited Findings
**Tourism and events (winter peak)**
- Dubai had 19.59m international overnight visitors in 2025, up 5% from 18.72m in 2024. December 2025 was the record month at 2.04m (+6% YoY), and the previous record was January 2025 at 1.94m. Hotel occupancy 80.7%. Western Europe 4.1m (about 21%); GCC+MENA 26% — [Gulf News, Feb 2026](https://gulfnews.com/business/tourism/dubai-tourism-hits-record-as-1959m-international-visitors-arrive-in-2025-1.500437381); [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/bynk1j3-dubai’s-tourism-industry-achieves-third-successive)
- Daily inflow into Dubai (commuters plus visitors) averaged about 1.81m in 2025/26 — [Khaleej Times, 30 July 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-population-surges-75-to-458-million-as-growth-rate-nears-record-high)

**Ramadan (Adjust "Ramadan app trends decoded", data Ramadan 2025, 28 Feb–30 Mar 2025, vs 2025 average)**
- UAE: overall installs +10%, games +24%, e-commerce +36%, shopping sub-vertical +42%. UAE overall sessions +9%, e-commerce sessions +101%, shopping sessions +111% — [Adjust, Jan 2026 (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-ramadan-app-trends-report.pdf); [Adjust resource page](https://www.adjust.com/resources/ebooks/ramadan-app-trends/)
- METAP proxy: installs +10% during Ramadan, +12% around Eid and +13% in the two weeks after. Food & drink installs −26% during Ramadan, then +16% at Eid. Utilities +26% during Ramadan and peaking at +43% after. Average session length 19.34 min before Ramadan vs 20.08 min during (+4%). Gaming sessions averaged 35.6 min — [Adjust, Jan 2026](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-ramadan-app-trends-report.pdf)
- Sensor Tower: shopping downloads spike more than 30% above a typical day around Ramadan and Black Friday in KSA and the UAE. UAE finance downloads show muted salary-cycle patterns (May is a strong month), unlike KSA's month-end spikes. 61.41% of marketers see new seasonal windows — [Sensor Tower & Bidease, Oct 2025](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/ST_2025-Middle-East-App-Growth-Report.pdf)
- Dulook DXB prayer app: +185% usage in Ramadan — [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/1749dxv-dubai’s-dulook-dxb-prayer-app-nears-one-million)
- Ramadan 2026 framed as a "nocturnal revival" of shopping and screen time after iftar (survey expectations, not measured data) — [Gulf News, 2026](https://gulfnews.com/business/retail/ramadan-2026-in-uae-how-shopping-screen-time-travel-change-after-iftar-1.500409947)

**Summer**
- Many residents travel out in July as schools break up, and the return flow builds in late August (Dubai Airports' busiest days) — [The National, Aug 2023](https://www.thenationalnews.com/travel/2023/08/16/dubai-airports-busiest-days-august/)
- "Hundreds of thousands" stay through summer (qualitative) — [Sandy Times](https://sandytimes.ae/articles/5068-why-everyone-leaves-dubai-in-summer-and-why-some-people-love-staying)
- The resident count kept rising through summer 2026: 4,741,335 (30 July), 4,782,535 (end-August), 4,807,185 (29 Sept) — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-population-surges-75-to-458-million-as-growth-rate-nears-record-high); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai-population-rises-by-226885-since-start-of-2026-1.500692623)

### Inferences
- Calendar for launches and campaigns: Ramadan (roughly mid-Feb to mid-March in 2026; about early February in 2027, based on the Hijri calendar, approximate) favours religious, gaming, shopping and utility apps and late-night engagement, but it is a weak window for food and dieting apps. Eid and the two weeks after bring the highest install lift. September (return from summer, back to school, new arrivals) and January (New Year resolutions plus peak tourist season) suit health, habit and education apps. July–August is a likely trough for local-services demand and paid UA efficiency, but a good window for travel-companion, diaspora and "summer at home" content.
- Tourist volume (about 1.6m a month average, 2m+ in December) is a sizable transient audience for event, dining and tourism apps. These users rarely subscribe, so they suit transactional or affiliate models.

### Gaps
- No quantitative measure of the summer exodus (share of residents abroad in July–August) and no UAE app-usage index by month. No UAE-specific Ramadan time-spent (hours) data. Monthly 2025 tourism figures other than December and January were not retrieved, so the summer trough is inferred, not measured.
